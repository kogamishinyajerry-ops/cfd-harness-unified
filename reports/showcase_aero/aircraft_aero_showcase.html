NACA0012 RANS Sweep — Pure-Aerodynamic Vertical

2D wing-section lift polar, run end-to-end through the cfd-harness workbench adapter on Foundation OpenFOAM 11.

DEC-V61-225 runnable-coverage = 1 OpenFOAM 11 (Foundation) foamRun -solver incompressibleFluid 2026-06-03

What this is. A four-point angle-of-attack sweep of a NACA 0012 airfoil, solved as a steady incompressible RANS problem at flight Reynolds number (Re = 3×106). Each operating point was driven end-to-end through the workbench's FoamAgentExecutor.execute() adapter against the live cfd-openfoam Foundation-OF11 container — not hand-run on the command line.

Honest scope: this is a 2D wing-section, NOT a 3D aircraft. It is the canonical building block of aircraft external aerodynamics (a single span station), solved in the airfoil's chord plane. There is no fuselage, no wingtip, no 3D induced drag, no span effects, no compressibility (single incompressible/kinematic regime). Treat the numbers as a 2D sectional polar, nothing more.

Why it matters here. This is the first time the pure-aerodynamic vertical was proven runnable-coverage = 1 through the adapter: the workbench's ESI-style request (simpleFoam) was reconciled to the Foundation-OF11 invocation foamRun -solver incompressibleFluid (the W3.2b / DEC-V61-225 reconciliation), and the full request → container-run → harvested-coefficients loop closed for all four points. ESI solver names like simpleFoam / chtMultiRegionSimpleFoam do not exist in this image; the adapter maps to the Foundation module solver. The solver-log banner below confirms the actual executable.

Honest read of the Cl≈0 point (AoA 0°). A symmetric airfoil at zero incidence produces zero net lift; the harvested Cl is −1.6×10−8, i.e. numerically zero (sign is meaningless at this magnitude). The function-object's "Cl drift over last 100 iterations" reads as a huge percentage precisely because it is a relative drift on a near-zero base — the absolute value is flat and the Cd drift is 3×10−4%. We report it as Cl = 0, not as a real lift contribution.

4 · Per-run convergence & near-wall resolution

RunAoAIterations Solver verdictPost-fix recompute y+ min / avg / maxy+ advisoryNotes
Two points hit the iteration cap (8° and 12°). At AoA 8° and 12° the solver reached the endTime cap of 8000 iterations without printing its own "SIMPLE solution converged" verdict (solver_declared_converged = false). An independent post-run recomputation of the convergence criterion from the final residuals (all ≤ 7×10−7 on p, ≤ 2.4×10−7 on omega) and the coefficient drift (Cl/Cd drift < 5×10−4% over the last 100 iterations) finds them effectively converged — so we mark them capped / recompute-converged, not a clean solver-declared convergence. The 12° coefficients were additionally harvested after the driver process was killed (harvested_post_driver_kill = true); the artifacts are intact and the coefficients come straight from the function-object output, but we flag it so no one mistakes it for an untouched clean run. AoA 0° and 4° are clean solver-declared convergences (0° at 6645 iters, 4° at 4010 iters).

All four runs pass the y+ advisory (wall-resolved, max y+ from 37 at 0° rising to 103 at 12° as the suction-side gradients steepen). y+ rising toward the wall-function boundary at the highest AoA is expected and is reported, not hidden.

5 · What is NOT claimed

  • No 3D / finite-wing effects, no induced drag, no wingtip vortices — this is a 2D section.
  • No experimental validation is asserted on this page; the polar is internally consistent and physically plausible (slope below 2π/rad, monotone Cd rise, pre-stall), but a Ladson/TM-4074 cross-check is a separate exercise.
  • The 8° and 12° points are iteration-capped (recompute-converged), not solver-declared converged; the 12° point was harvested post-driver-kill. Both are flagged above.
  • Every number on this page is a harvested run value. Nothing is interpolated, fitted-then-reported as data, or fabricated.
