- Your refinement only weakly refutes **streamwise truncation error in that one grid family**. It does not refute wrong freestream `k/omega`, OpenFOAM SST-equation mismatch, wall treatment, wall-normal under-resolution near the LE, or model-activation differences. NASA explicitly warns that verification only converges if flow conditions and BCs match, and that incompressible solvers may not exactly match the M=0.2 reference. Sources: [TMR flat plate intro](https://tmbwg.github.io/turbmodels/flatplate.html), [TMR notes](https://tmbwg.github.io/turbmodels/noteonrunning.html).

- NASA does acknowledge the **leading-edge singularity** and says local anomalous behavior exists near `x=0`; it also says SST turbulence activation differs very near the LE, specifically `0 < x < 0.01`. That supports skepticism about grading the first few points. It does **not** support silently moving the cutoff to `0.03` after failures persist to `0.027`. Source: [SST-Vm expected results](https://tmbwg.github.io/turbmodels/flatplate_sst.html).

- I do not see a NASA/TMR rule saying “exclude `x < 0.03`.” TMR shows the whole-plate `Cf` curve, but its quantitative convergence examples use integrated drag and `Cf` at `x=0.97008`, far downstream. So your per-point 10% gate starting at `0.01` was your own gate, and moving it to exactly past the observed failure band is cherry-pick shaped.

- The cheap honest fixes/tests are not exhausted: set NASA SST freestream values exactly (`Tu=0.039%`, `mu_t/mu=0.009`, equivalent `k=1.125 U^2/Re_L`, `omega=125 U/L`), confirm no wall functions and correct `omegaWallFunction`/low-Re treatment, check OpenFOAM’s SST equations against TMR, and try lower first-cell height. TMR notes OpenFOAM SST historically did not match its equations without code changes. Source: [OpenFOAM issues](https://tmbwg.github.io/turbmodels/openfoam_issues.html).

- A defensible V&V scope would say: “primary gate is developed region, e.g. `x >= 0.03`, because LE singular/activation behavior is out of scope,” **before** seeing results, and report `x < 0.03` as a known deviation. As written, this is post-hoc gate movement with plausible physics attached.
